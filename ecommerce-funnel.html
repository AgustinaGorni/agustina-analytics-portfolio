Where ecommerce shoppers actually drop off

Checkout usually gets blamed for lost sales. On this dataset, the real leak is one step earlier — between viewing a product and adding it to the cart. Here's the process from raw event data to a tracking plan built to diagnose it.

Dataset & method

Built entirely on the public Google Merchandise Store GA4 sample dataset (BigQuery), covering Nov 2020 – Jan 2021. Queried in BigQuery, analyzed in Python, and translated into an Adobe Analytics / Customer Journey Analytics tracking plan. No client or employer data used.

The funnel

19.8% of viewers add to cart — the rest of the funnel converts fine

Once a user adds a product to their cart, the funnel holds up reasonably well: 74.5% continue to checkout, and 73.2% of those who add payment info complete the purchase. The bottleneck sits earlier, on the product page itself.

Funnel by unique users · Nov 2020 – Jan 2021 · GA4 public dataset, queried via BigQuery
Why this matters for the tracking plan: if the objective were framed around checkout (the common assumption), the resulting instrumentation would miss the actual problem entirely. The data reframes the objective before a single eVar gets defined.

Business objective

Reduce the view_item → add_to_cart drop-off

Identify where in the product page experience purchase intent is lost, and whether that varies by traffic source, device, or product/category.

  • At what point in the PDP visit is the user lost — before engaging with the CTA, after engaging but not completing, or due to stock/price?
  • Does drop-off behavior vary by acquisition channel or device?
  • Are there products or categories with consistently lower add-to-cart rates?
  • Does the path to the PDP — search, category browse, or promotion — influence conversion to cart?

Tracking plan

From GA4 events to Adobe Analytics success events

The full spec — including eVars, props, implementation rules, QA plan, and team coordination — is in the downloadable document. The additions below are the part that matters most for a lead role: instrumentation that GA4 doesn't capture by default, proposed specifically to diagnose this drop-off rather than just confirm it.

  • CTA visible — confirms the add-to-cart button actually entered the viewport, separating "never saw it" from "saw it and passed."
  • Stock/availability shown — correlates drop-off with low-availability products.
  • Failed add-to-cart attempt — CTA clicks that don't result in a successful cart update.
  • Scroll depth / active time on PDP — separates immediate exits from exits after evaluation.
  • Price tier at time of view — allows cross-referencing conversion by price range.
